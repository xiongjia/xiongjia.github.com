## What this proves

A resizable layout is three separate problems: measuring the drag, storing the result, and making the
panes good citizens afterwards. `packages/panels` solves them in ~180 lines with no dependency — and
the third one is where B8 pays off: every pane is a `@container`, so the cards inside reflow on the
pane width and never on the window width.

## Decisions

- **Percentages, not pixels.** A stored `320px` is wrong on the next viewport. A percentage survives a
  window resize and needs no `ResizeObserver` to stay correct.
- **`setPointerCapture` on the separator.** Without capture, a fast drag leaves the 6px handle, the
  events stop, and the pointer-up lands on whatever is underneath. Capture also suppresses text
  selection during the drag without a `user-select: none` on `<body>`.
- **Clamping on read and on write.** A hand-edited `localStorage` value, or a future change to
  `min`/`max`, must not be able to produce a zero-width pane. `clampSize` runs both when the stored
  value is parsed and when a new one is set.
- **The default size is a prop of the hook, not of the component.** `useSplitSize` owns persistence
  and bounds; `SplitPane` is presentational and takes `size` + `onSizeChange`. That split is what
  makes the same component usable for a persisted IDE layout and for a throwaway demo.
- **`flexBasis` percentages with `flex-grow/shrink: 0`.** Letting flexbox grow the panes would fight
  the stored size; pinning both to 0 makes the basis the only thing that decides.

## Composition

The nested vertical split inside the right pane is not a special case in the component: a `SplitPane`
is a normal box, so a second one fits inside a pane. Inverting the nested percentage
(`onSizeChange={(next) => setSize(100 - next)}`) is the demo's decision, not the library's — the pane
above simply wants a different anchor.

## Pitfalls

- `@container` on the pane means `overflow: auto` has to be on the pane too. Without it, a pane whose
  content is wider than the basis pushes the layout instead of scrolling.
- Persisting on every `pointermove` writes ~60 times a second. It is harmless for a single key, but a
  debounce belongs here the moment several splits are saved together.
- Double-click to reset is a nicety, not the accessibility path: the keyboard reset is `Enter`, and
  both call the same `onSizeChange(50)`.
- The separator is 6px wide for the pointer but its focus ring is drawn inset; an outline on a 6px
  element is otherwise clipped by the neighbouring pane.
